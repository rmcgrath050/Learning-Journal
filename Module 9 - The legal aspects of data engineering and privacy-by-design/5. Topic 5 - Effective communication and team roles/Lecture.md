<b>Scenario 2: Stakeholder Alignment in a Retail Predictive Analytics Project</b>

1. What strategies in the scenarios were effective, and why?
- The workshop as it aligned exceptions of everyone involved in project 
- Regular information provided via dashboards to help stakeholders make decisions
- The one to one meetings about addressing concerns feeds into trust and gives more courage 121 as they may not wanted to have shared this as part of wider group 


2. What were the weaknesses of these strategies, and how could they be
improved?

- Tailored communication is missing as store managers found the dashboards too technical. High level summaries are more appropriate for executives. Could use simpler language, clear visualisations etc.
- Communication frequency with the feedback, would recommend increasing communication with stakeholders about issues via a feedback tracking system 


4. Could alternative engagement strategies have worked better for certain
stakeholders?

- Store managers: Short training sessions, demonstrations, and simple mobile-friendly dashboards would probably work better than highly technical reports.
- Supply chain managers: Detailed analytics workshops and more sophisticated dashboards could be appropriate because they are likely to need deeper operational information.
- Marketing team: Regular cross-functional meetings could help them understand how inventory predictions relate to promotions, customer demand, and campaigns.
- All stakeholders: A feedback portal, regular Q&A sessions, and stakeholder surveys could provide additional ways to communicate concerns.

6. What role does clear communication play in improving stakeholder
engagement?

Clear communication is essential because stakeholders need to understand what the analytics are showing, why it matters, what decisions need to be made, and how their feedback is being used.

In this scenario, clearer communication could have prevented some of the problems. For example, explaining dashboard information in business rather than technical language would make it easier for store managers to act on the insights. Similarly, communicating the status of feedback—even when it cannot immediately be implemented—would reduce frustration and create realistic expectations.

Overall, effective stakeholder engagement requires two-way, timely, and audience-appropriate communication. The project should not simply provide information to stakeholders; it should also listen to them, respond to their concerns, and demonstrate how their input influences decisions.

1. What strategies in the scenarios were effective, and why?
-	Align goals - A variety of stakeholders in this with high influence and interest, so making a governance committee was a good call.

2. What were the weaknesses of these strategies, and how could they be
improved?
-	Compliance reps perhaps weren’t on committee as unclear on when to provide input, or weekly email update did not invite any feedback.
-	Putting training workshops close to launch date does not leave much time for managers to provide feedback as well as leaving them feeling uninformed.

3. Could alternative engagement strategies have worked better for certain
stakeholders?
-	Stakeholder mapping could have been utilised to make sure that everyone that needed to give input was part of the committee if this did not happen. Alternatively compliance reps and other stakeholders with influence could have had a regular meeting where they had their chance to provide input.
