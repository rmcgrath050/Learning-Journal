# L5DE 9.6: Project reports and presentations

The project evaluation report and presentation are your chance to demonstrate not only what you did, but why it mattered and what you learned!

- The presentation is a short, structured talk (often 10 – 15 minutes) where you summarise key aspects of the project.
- The Q&A gives assessors a chance to probe your reasoning, decisions, and reflections
- Report should say about - it tells the story of your work, your decisions, and your growth.


## Essential components 
- Executive summary: clear and concise overview.
- Objectives & scope: what the project aimed to achieve.
- Methodology: technical approach, tools, and frameworks used.
- Implementation: description of the build or pipeline.
- Results: outcomes and measurements
- Evaluation: strengths, weaknesses, improvements
- Reflection: what you learned, professional growth, links to KSBs.

<br>

eg .<i>
“The pipeline processed 2.4M rows of transactional data daily with a 99.8% success rate. This met the business requirement for near - real - time reporting and demonstrated my ability to optimise batch processes.”
</i>

## The anatomy of a strong presentation

- Keep slides simple: use visuals, diagrams, and bullet points sparingly.
- Follow a clear flow: Introduction → Problem → Solution → Results → Reflection.
- Use slides to support your message, not to read from.
- Finish with a strong conclusion: why this project mattered


<br>
This section prepares you for the types of questions assessors may ask - testing your understanding, decision-making, and ability to reflect - and offers practical strategies to respond effectively and link back to your evidence.

<br>
- Clarification: “Why did you choose this method?”
- Justification: “What alternatives did you consider?”
- Reflection: “What would you do differently?”
- Strategy: Use STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) or simply answer directly and link back to your evidence
- The presentation is a summary. It should highlight the key points, not repeat the whole report.

#### Example answers

- Measurable outcomes
- Business link
- Design decision explained
- Reflection included

<i>“I designed a pipeline that ingested 2.4M daily transaction
records into a SQL data store with a 99.8% success rate. This
met the SLA for near-real-time reporting. I evaluated a
streaming option but discounted it due to unnecessary cost.
From this I learned how to optimise batch ingestion and justify
design trade-offs.”</i>
